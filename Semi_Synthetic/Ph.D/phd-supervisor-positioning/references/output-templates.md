# Output Templates

## 1. Detailed Academic Research Profile / Prospective Supervision Dossier

Target length: 2–4 pages of normal document text.

Default title:
**Research Profile and Doctoral Research Direction**

Optional subtitle:
**Prospective PhD Supervision Dossier**

Recommended structure:

### Research identity
A concise paragraph defining the researcher's present focus and distinctive combination of experience.

### Research trajectory
Explain how prior work developed into the current doctoral direction. Focus on intellectual and methodological continuity, not a job-by-job chronology.

### Current doctoral research agenda
State the current PhD topic, research problem, research questions/objectives, and what is completed versus still under development.

### Methodological and technical competencies
Group capabilities into 4–6 research-relevant clusters. Prefer evidence and application context over long software lists.

### Selected research outputs and contributions
Select publications/projects most relevant to the target professor. State status and venue accurately. Do not infer author role.

### Data, annotation, and research operations
Include leadership in annotation, evaluation, quality control, dataset construction, or research coordination when relevant.

### Teaching and academic experience
Include only the most relevant teaching and mentoring background.

### Alignment with Professor [Name] / [Lab]
Identify specific overlap and complementarity using target-source evidence.

### Potential joint doctoral directions
Give 2–4 concrete research directions framed as possibilities. Label as proposed directions, not commitments.

### Research links
ORCID / Google Scholar / GitHub / personal academic page as available.

## 2. One-page Research Profile

Use this compact structure:

**[Name]**  
**PhD Researcher — [current research identity]**

**Current focus**  
3–4 lines on current doctoral research.

**Research strengths**  
4–6 concise bullets grouped around methods/capabilities, not generic soft skills.

**Selected evidence**  
3–5 strongest publications/projects/roles.

**Current doctoral direction**  
2–4 bullets on research questions or planned contributions, clearly marking proposed work.

**Why this may align with Professor [Name]**  
2–4 evidence-backed bullets.

**Links**  
ORCID | Scholar | GitHub

After the table, add:
- **Best-fit intersection:** 1 concise paragraph.
- **Complementary value:** what the user adds that is not simply duplication.
- **Fit caveats:** missing evidence, topic drift, availability unknown, or methodological mismatch.
- **Possible joint directions:** 2–4 clearly speculative ideas.

## 4. First-contact Email

Target length: 180–250 words unless the user asks otherwise.

**Subject:** Prospective PhD Supervision — [specific research intersection]

Dear Professor [Surname],

I am [current academic status] at [institution], working on [current research focus]. My recent work has focused on [2–3 evidence-backed themes].

I am contacting you because your work on [specific professor theme/paper/project] closely intersects with my current direction in [specific overlap]. [One sentence on a second overlap or complementary strength.]

My doctoral work currently examines [concise research problem]. Relevant experience includes [2–3 strongest items: publication/project/data leadership/technical-methodological evidence].

I am currently exploring prospective supervision / a supervision transfer [include this clause only when appropriate and approved by the user], and I would value the opportunity to discuss whether my research direction could fit within your group. I have attached a concise research profile/CV and would be glad to share my proposal or a more detailed research plan.

Best regards,  
[Name]  
[Institution]  
[Selected links]

### Email rules
- Personalize with 1–2 specific target-professor references.
- Do not flatter generically.
- Do not explain conflict with a current supervisor.
- Do not overstuff the email with publication lists.
- Keep the ask modest: discussion of fit, not an assumption of acceptance.
