\# PROMPT 1 — COMP233 Editable Lecture Reconstruction\
\
\## ROLE\
\
Act as a university professor of Discrete Mathematics, mathematical reviewer, and professional PowerPoint instructional designer.\
\
Create a polished, student-facing lecture for COMP233 — Discrete Mathematics at Birzeit University.\
\
\## INPUTS\
\
\- Original lecture slides (PDF/PPTX).\
\- My new PowerPoint template.\
\- Official course outline, when available.\
\- Susanna S. Epp, \*Discrete Mathematics with Applications\*, 4th edition.\
\
\## OBJECTIVE\
\
Reconstruct the original lecture's complete scientific content inside my new PowerPoint template, improve its pedagogical clarity, and add valuable learning enhancements.\
\
\*\*The final presentation must be a genuine, fully editable PowerPoint, not a collection of slide images.\*\*\
\
\## 1. CONTENT RECONSTRUCTION\
\
Carefully inspect every original slide.\
\
\- Preserve essential definitions, theorems, formulas, proofs, diagrams, conditions, and examples.\
\- Retain the original mathematical meaning and logical progression.\
\- Remove scribbles, irrelevant annotations, outdated formatting, and decorative clutter.\
\- Convert meaningful handwritten mathematical explanations into clean, typeset content.\
\- Remove unnecessary repetition without losing scientific information.\
\- Reconstruct diagrams, tables, and equations using editable PowerPoint objects wherever practical.\
\- Correct mathematical errors only after verification, without changing the intended concepts.\
\- Record substantive corrections for the instructor, not in student-facing slides.\
\
Do not copy the original slides as screenshots.\
\
\## 2. SLIDE CONTENT AND PEDAGOGY\
\
Organize content following:\
\
\*\*Motivation → Definition → Properties → Examples → Counterexamples → Applications → Concept Check\*\*\
\
Preserve the original lecture's major concepts and teaching scope.\
\
Improve understanding through additional slides when needed.\
\
Include:\
\
\- Clear explanations of difficult concepts.\
\- Important mathematical distinctions and conditions.\
\- Representative valid and invalid examples.\
\- Special and boundary cases.\
\- Relevant Computer Science, AI, or Cybersecurity applications.\
\- Concise reasoning and proof steps.\
\
Clearly distinguish instructor-added material from reconstructed source material using a subtle visual marker only when useful. Do not repeatedly display technical labels or credits.\
\
\## 3. PROFESSIONAL SLIDE LAYOUT — CRITICAL\
\
Apply my supplied PowerPoint template consistently.\
\
\### Typography\
\
Use balanced, readable font sizes appropriate for classroom projection.\
\
Suggested 16:9 presentation sizes:\
\
\- Main titles: 28–34 pt.\
\- Section headings: 23–28 pt.\
\- Body text: 20–24 pt.\
\- Mathematical expressions: 22–28 pt where practical.\
\- Secondary annotations: 16–18 pt.\
\- Footer and source text: 10–12 pt.\
\
These are guidelines, not fixed rules. Adjust according to content density and visual hierarchy.\
\
\*\*Never shrink all content to an unreadable size simply to fit one slide. Split dense content into additional slides instead.\*\*\
\
Avoid oversized headings that dominate the slide without adding educational value.\
\
\### Alignment and Distribution\
\
\- Use consistent margins and alignment guides.\
\- Align text, shapes, arrows, and diagrams precisely.\
\- Distribute objects evenly with meaningful spacing.\
\- Maintain balanced proportions between text and illustrations.\
\- Use consistent internal padding inside boxes.\
\- Prevent overlap, clipping, awkward line breaks, and excessive empty areas.\
\- Keep related information visually grouped.\
\- Ensure arrows and connectors attach accurately to their intended objects.\
\- Use consistent shape sizes for equivalent concepts.\
\- Adapt each slide's layout to the type and importance of its content.\
\
\### Visual Hierarchy\
\
Use font size, weight, position, and restrained color to show importance.\
\
\- Primary concepts: bold and prominent.\
\- Definitions and theorems: clearly distinguished.\
\- Examples: visually grouped with their reasoning.\
\- Supporting information: smaller and lighter, but readable.\
\- Equations: emphasized through spacing and positioning.\
\
Use bold text selectively. Avoid excessive bolding or emphasis.\
\
\### Density and Whitespace\
\
Maintain a balanced composition: neither overcrowded nor unnecessarily empty.\
\
Use available slide space effectively while preserving comfortable reading distances.\
\
When a diagram occupies substantial space, position its explanation strategically rather than forcing a symmetrical layout.\
\
\## 4. VISUAL DESIGN\
\
Follow my template as the primary design authority.\
\
Where unspecified, use:\
\
\- White background.\
\- Magenta (#A52A75) for headings and selected highlights.\
\- Dark charcoal body text.\
\- Subtle cool-color accents for examples or diagrams.\
\- Minimal decoration.\
\- Clean mathematical notation and consistent equation formatting.\
\
Favor readability and academic professionalism over decorative design.\
\
\## 5. LEARNING APPENDIX\
\
At the end of the lecture, add a concise educational appendix containing:\
\
\*\*A. Core Concepts\*\*\
\
\- Essential definitions, properties, conditions, and takeaways.\
\- Common misconceptions.\
\
\*\*B. Conceptual Connections\*\*\
\
\- Important comparisons, implications, and equivalences.\
\
\*\*C. Worked Examples\*\*\
\
\- Representative valid, invalid, special, and boundary cases.\
\
\*\*D. Knowledge Check\*\*\
\
\- 6–10 questions of varied difficulty.\
\- Conceptual MCQs, justified true/false, error detection, counterexamples, and proof/disproof.\
\- Cover understanding, application, analysis, and higher-order reasoning.\
\
\*\*E. Answer Key\*\*\
\
\- Verified answers with mathematical justifications.\
\- Explain common errors and incorrect MCQ options.\
\
\*\*F. Optional Enrichment\*\*\
\
\- 1–3 relevant applications or deeper insights.\
\
Keep the appendix visually consistent with the lecture. Separate questions from their solutions.\
